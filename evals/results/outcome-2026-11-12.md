## Synthesis

On the bug the user reported, the two variants tie.
The fix is the same in all four, and all four prove it on the real CLI with a spec that fails before the fix.

The judge's two-point gap comes from one input class, a bill of about $76 trillion.
deep-mode's verifiers found that edge, and both deep-mode candidates added an input cap for it.
I score all four 9/10 by my own reading.
A and B lose a point for the README note. C and D lose one for unrequested behavior changes.
D's $1 billion cap now refuses a $2 billion total that the old CLI split correctly.
C's cap changes the error text a bad `--tip` gets.

Three things weaken the judge's margin.

- The judge is the same model family as the verifiers C and D started, and the deciding finding is the one those verifiers raised.
  That is the model-bias case the Eval playbook warns about.
- The rubric's "every split adds up" has no realistic input range, so a trillion-dollar total counts the same as a dinner bill.
- Two candidates per variant on one task is too small to separate the variants.

What the run does show is measured.

- deep-mode cost 5 to 12 times more ($1.02 and $2.24 against $0.18 and $0.22) and made 4 to 10 times the tool calls.
- deep-mode's candidates sized the fix as S and followed the S lane.
  They used one restate, a worktree, one verifier from another model family, a commit, and a merge at the verified head.
- deep-mode's candidates left a committed, merged change. The plain ones left uncommitted edits.
- Containment held in both variants with the global contain hook off.

## Recommendation

Do not claim from this run that deep-mode fixes small bugs better.
On this size S bug it reached the same fix at 5 to 12 times the cost.
The extra spend bought a cross-family check that found a real but far-fetched edge case, and two unrequested behavior changes.
Keep the variant. Nothing regressed, and its S lane did what the skill says.
But treat the S-lane verifier's cost on one-file fixes as the finding to act on.
One option is a cheaper verifier tier for S, or a brief that tells the verifier to stay within realistic inputs.
Next run, use an M-size task where the playbook should matter, three or more candidates per variant, and a judge from a third model family or two judges from different families.
