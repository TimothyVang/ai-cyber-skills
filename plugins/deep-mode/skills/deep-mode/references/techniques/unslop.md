# unslop

**Use when:** writing or editing any prose (replies, docs, PRs, commits, logs). Scan for the tells, rewrite, preserve meaning and tone. Rule numbers are stable ids.

- **Content.** 3 superficial -ing tails ("highlighting…", "ensuring…") → delete or substantiate. 5 vague attributions ("experts believe") → name the source or delete.
- **Language.** 7 AI vocabulary (additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, vibrant) → plain words. 8 "serves as/stands as/boasts/features" → "is/has". 9 "not just X, but Y" → state it. 10 forced triads → natural count. 11 synonym cycling → one name. 12 false ranges ("from X to Y" off-scale) → list directly.
- **Style.** 13 no em dashes (nor en dashes, parens, or hyphen substitutes as dashes): period or comma. 14 colons only before lists/examples, not as connectors. 15 no bolding every noun. 16 no inline-header lists restating the line ("**Performance:** Performance improved") → prose; a bold lead-in ending in a period followed by new detail is fine. 17 sentence-case headings. 18 no decorative emojis. 19 straight quotes.
- **Chat artifacts.** 20 "I hope this helps!", "Certainly!", "Found the smoking gun!" → remove. 22 sycophancy → answer directly.
- **Filler.** 23 "in order to" → "to"; "due to the fact that" → "because"; drop "it is important to note that". 24 stacked hedges → "may". 25 generic conclusions → specific facts or plans.
- **Jargon.** 26 abstract metaphor nouns: substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage, nexus, primitive (noun), harness (metaphor), surface ("API surface"), bedrock, scaffolding (metaphor), modality, paradigm, gold-plating, ratchet (metaphor), evacuate, endgame, north star, flywheel → the concrete word.
- **Plain speech.** 27 say what it does, not how it feels: name the mechanism or number; if a sentence can't become an instruction, fact, or number, or could appear unchanged in another project's docs, cut it. 28 split dense sentences, one idea each. 29 active voice; name the actor. 30 cut adverbs, use the number or a stronger verb. 31 plain word (use, help, many, if). 32 no mannered prose: aphorisms, rhetorical fragments, personified code, figurative verbs. 33 no over-compression: whole sentences with articles and verbs; spell out arrows and abbreviations.

**Hard rule.** Meaning survives; only the tells go.
